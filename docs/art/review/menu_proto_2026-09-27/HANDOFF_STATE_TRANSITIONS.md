### C. Integration decisions still open

1. **How the hybrid mounts in MenuShell.** Today each page is a Control
   tree in its own SubViewport, shown flat on its wall. The hybrid is 3D
   hardware standing off the walls:
   - modules slide toward the eye;
   - cables run round the corner posts;
   - the Journal's link crosses two walls;
   - its words are `Label3D`s placed to be seen at their page pixels.

   The prototype puts each wall's parts in the box's own `World3D` and
   hit-tests with the same wall-plane ray as `MenuShell._page_hit`. It
   shows that clicks stay exact (the tapes click every target through the
   real input path). The alternative is Control pages for words, with a 3D
   layer in front aligned to page pixels. That cannot move words with a
   module that slides. Production's call; the art lane recommends the
   prototype's way.
2. **Renderer.** The prototype runs GL Compatibility. Production's
   `project.godot` sets no rendering method, so it runs Forward+.
   - **Remove or re-tune the tone factors under Forward+.** The
     Compatibility renderer ignores a light's cull mask, so the Map
     miniature's light also falls on the Map's wall and, glancing, on the
     Journal's. The prototype tones pale surfaces there to compensate
     (`Shell.SHADE`: Map 0.74, Journal 0.86; `FaceMap._build_panel` 0.74).
     If Forward+ honours the mask, those surfaces render too dark.
   - **Re-measure §1's contrast figures** after that.
   - **Check the lighting, shadows and MSAA** of the hardware under
     Production's renderer.
3. **The equip flow in the hybrid's words and places:**
   - where PENDING, REFUSED (and its message), NOT SENT and LOST read: the
     module's window, the key's window, the readout's foot;
   - what "back to the save" becomes once an equip is sent (another
     request);
   - how a key is cleared;
   - what the actions do offline (`BridgeClient.can_send()`).
4. **The consumable's states:** where the six states and their texts
   read. Candidates are the Q key's window, the module's window, and the
   readout.
5. **Parts of EquipmentFace the hybrid has no place for yet:**
   - search, and sort (`EquipmentQuery.grid`, `sorted_items`);
   - NEW marks (`EquipmentSeen`);
   - the link and bridge lines;
   - the HISTORY ▸/▾ toggle (the hybrid always shows history, placed by
     room);
   - the footer.

   The hybrid's rack is per key and in a fixed order. Decide which of
   these carry over, and where.
6. **ABANDON ZONE's confirmation** on the PAUSED board, which the
   prototype does not draw, and the board's Hub layout.
7. **The Esc/B back ladder (§6.3) and input during a turn (§6.4).** The
   owner decided both in the second ruling. They are not built yet: the
   prototype backs out on B but keeps Production's Esc, which closes at
   once, and it drops input during a turn, as Production does. Production
   writes both, and chooses which keyboard key, if any, closes at once
   from any depth.
8. **Whether MOTION applies at once.** The prototype applies it at once;
   MenuShell reads it only on `open()`.
9. **Where the menu's cues live,** and their levels. The options are
   Production's `Tones` (Production's file) or a menu-owned bank. The art
   lane has **not listened** to the cues: they are first settings.
10. **Held-key acceleration on the sliders** (§6.15). Production's HSlider
    moves one step per press.
11. **ALWAYS ON as the selector's sixth position.** It has no key and no
    binding; confirm this fits Production's five-key model.
12. **What the comparison compares against** while an equip is pending:
    the prototype compares against what is seated; with real requests,
    probably the bridge-confirmed occupant.
13. **720p text.** Body text is 2x, which is 16 page px, or about 13.8
    screen px at 1280 × 720. The quieter words were lifted in the fifth
    ruling. §6.5's legibility check stands.
14. **The provisional circuit symbols** (K, P, M), and SIGNAL's near-twin
    `span_alignment` (Glyph handoff §4, §5).

### D. Untested behaviour

- **Hands-on use.** It has not been tried with a keyboard, mouse or
  controller by anybody. The owner's approval came from a phone video of
  the scripted tour.
- **The sound.** Its presence is checked (the cue log, and the tour's
  audio track); its levels and character are unheard by the art lane.
- **Live data.** The prototype has never been fed real bridge data:
  - the bridge going offline or coming back;
  - a snapshot landing mid-browse;
  - a request answered while another key is being read;
  - more items than the sample (13 on one key is the most tested).
- **Production's renderer (Forward+) and its SubViewport pages** (§C.1,
  §C.2).
- **Not pressed by any tape:**
  - KP Enter;
  - the Map's A and S (they share W's and D's code);
  - the Map's d-pad → (the same call as `]`);
  - pad A for SHOW ON THE MAP (the same call as Enter).
- **Not run at all:**
  - physical controller hardware, and non-Xbox layouts;
  - window resizing and focus loss;
  - resolutions other than 1280 × 720 and 1920 × 1080;
  - a long session.
- **Performance.** The readout is rebuilt whole on each selection (a few
  dozen `Label3D`s), and the rack on each scroll. It is smooth in the
  tapes, but has not been profiled on low-end hardware.

## 1. Rules every face keeps

- **One motion system.** Every move is a track on a property (`Kit.go`).
  - A new target for the same property replaces the running track, from
    wherever the value is now. Nothing is queued, and nothing waits for a
    move to finish.
  - Timers that start the next step ("rewind, then cast") are
    serial-guarded, so only the newest request ever runs.
- **Motion never delays input.** Every input acts in the frame it lands.
  Words are rebuilt whole at once, and hardware catches up: a detent, a
  slide, a knob.
- **Reduced motion keeps the same information and the same actions.**
  - Every track becomes a cut, and every timer fires at once.
  - Secondary motion holds still: the gate pulse, and the link's cast.
  - It is driven by Production's own option, Settings → MOTION. OFF is
    `motion_intensity` 0.
- **Sound is information, not motion.** Reduced motion leaves every cue as
  it is. MASTER VOLUME sets them, and 0 silences them.
- **The mouse never chases.**
  - Hover only lights: a key's ground, a module's face, a Journal entry, a
    room. It never moves, grows or reorders anything.
  - A click acts on the thing under the pointer, where it is.
  - Selecting never moves a click target: the rack's rows, the keys'
    windows and the Settings rows stay put. Only a scroll moves rows, a
    whole row at a time.
- **Words never squash.** No text is ever scaled to animate, and
  `state().text_scale_min` is exactly 1 in rapid selection. Words placed
  off the wall are scaled on purpose, so that they are seen at their page
  size, and record that scale as their own.
- **Prompts follow the device last used.**
  - A key, a mouse button or mouse motion over 2 px selects keyboard and
    mouse. A pad button, or a stick past 0.4, selects the pad.
  - The readout's own control follows too: ENTER, or the pad's south face.
  - Every symbol has a readable text fallback (Glyph handoff §2).
- **No text below 3:1 against what is behind it on screen.**

## 2. Equipment — the cabinet and the grafted rack

**Composition.** Original station hardware, with a salvaged extension
grafted in:

- **The inspection window** (40, 108, 1200 × 280; divider at x 640) reads
  the item being inspected.
  - **Left:** where it stands with the key, its family and Mk, its name,
    DOES, the description, USE and COST.
  - **Right:** what changes if it goes on the key ("IF ON RMB, IN PLACE
    OF …", Production's `comparison` set as a table aligned on the arrow),
    then its history.
  - **At the foot, in the same place always:** the one action.
- **The key selector** is the cabinet's oldest part: a bakelite knob at
  (100, 572) with a pointer.
  - It has six positions on an arc: the five keys (`SLOT_NAMES`, with
    `SlotKeycaps`) and ALWAYS ON, which has no key.
  - Each position shows its keycap, its title and a backlit window: what
    is on the key, EMPTY, PREVIEW: <NAME>, a consumable's charges, or
    NO KEY · <n>.
- **The brass bus**, its terminal, a lead and an adapter board wire the
  rack back into the cabinet. A ribbon joins the board to the backplane.
- **The rack is salvaged.**
  - The cabinet's old bay has its frame kept on the top and the left, and
    cut off at x 1004: a bright cut face, then empty rivet holes.
  - Inside it: an aluminium adapter plate, and a paper-phenolic backplane
    on brass standoffs, running out to the wall's edge.
  - The modules plug into an edge connector: 9 rows, 28 px apart from
    y 456, each 25 px tall. A slide pot at x 1188 shows the scroll.
  - Its second ribbon leaves at y 650 and runs round the corner into
    Settings.

**Rapid selection.** The words never squash, and two readouts never
overlap: the old one is freed the moment the new one is built. The tapes
check, mid-flight in a run of presses:
- `state().text_scale_min` is 1;
- `state().compositions` is 1;
- the readout already reads the new item.

### Scrolling

- The rack shows whole rows, never part of one, so it needs no clipping.
- The keyboard and pad keep the selection in view. The wheel and the
  right stick move only the rack.
- "N MORE ABOVE" and "N MORE BELOW" are silk-screened on the backplane.
  `state().holes` stays 0.

### Ordering

The saved occupant comes first, then fold order. A preview never reorders
the rack, and selecting never moves it.

### The readout's fitting rules

- Every block is placed, and none is dropped.
- The left column takes the largest sizes that fit, trying the name and
  DOES at 5x/3x, 4x/3x, 5x/2x, 4x/2x, then 3x/2x. The name is never below
  3x.
- The history goes under the comparison, else under the left column,
  else the table closes up to an 18 px pitch.
- A refusal's words are wrapped whole at the foot.
- `state().focus.fits` is checked for every item on RMB in the stress
  sample. The longest name, 60 characters, sits in full on two lines at
  3x; the longest description, 158 characters, is also set in full.

### Data bindings

## 3. Map — LENS, in the riveted port

**The port.** The window (40, 90, 1200 × 604) sits in the enclosure's
heaviest frame: 14 px, riveted. The lens and everything inside it are
unchanged from the approved LENS.

### The lens

The lens is one camera state: `yaw, pitch, target, zoom, shift`. Every
input moves **the whole miniature**. No room is ever moved, spread or
enlarged for presentation. The geometry is ZoneController's committed
layout, and MapFace's own connector points.

### States

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| OVERVIEW | Nothing picked. Framed on the **known** rooms only. A way into the unknown runs out of frame as far as MapFace draws it, and no further. |
| PICKED | One room, framed in signal, with short answers on the glass. |
| EXPANDED | MapFace's own `detail_text`, on a **warm tag hung from the harness** over the port's right side. It is as tall as its words, with a SIGNAL edge while open. The lens slides the miniature aside (−170 page px) rather than covering it, and `view_rect()` then excludes the tag's side. |
| `floor` | −1 shows every floor. Otherwise it is an index into `MapFace.floors()`. |
| SHOWN | The Map was shown a Journal entry (`followed`), and keeps the view it had for BACK TO YOUR VIEW (`can_return`). |

### Transitions

- **Step place:** `[` `]`, or d-pad left/right. The lens moves in 0.38 s,
  cubic. The next press retargets it. Reduced: a cut.
- **Pick:** a still click. The pick closes in **around the pointer**: the
  point under the pointer stays put while the lens zooms. A drag of more
  than a few pixels is never a pick.
- **Orbit, pan and zoom:** arrows, WASD and `+`/`-`; or left-drag,
  right-drag and wheel; or the sticks and triggers.
- **Overview:** C, Home or Y.
- **Floors:** PgUp/PgDn, or d-pad up/down.
  - This uses Production's own banding (`MapFace.band_of_room`,
    `MinimapModel.FLOOR_STEP` 2.5). The first step shows *your* floor
    (`player_floor`), and stepping past either end shows every floor
    again.
  - The LENS **dims** the other floors, where Production hides them; see
    §6.6.
- **Expand and close:** Enter, or A. The lens shift animates in 0.30 s.
  Reduced: a cut.
- **Gate pulse:** 1.2 Hz, ±0.3. **Held still under reduced motion**;
  MapFace's own `_pulse()` does not do this (see §6.1).
- **Ordinary travel** (E, Q, the edge arrows, Tab) turns to the Map
  **exactly as it was left**: its lens, pick, detail and floor.
- **SHOW ON THE MAP** (Enter or A on a linked Journal entry) turns to the
  Map and brings what the entry names into view.
  - A place is picked. A passage is put at the window's centre, at a zoom
    that shows what is round it (at least the pick zoom), with every floor
    shown and the detail closed.
  - The lens glides 0.38 s; reduced, a cut.
  - The view it had is kept once, however many entries are shown after
    it.
- **Back:** Backspace or B steps back one level at a time:
  1. the open detail closes;
  2. the kept view is restored: its lens, pick, detail and floor;
  3. the pick is left for the overview.

  A click on BACK TO YOUR VIEW, on the glass, restores the kept view
  directly.

### Short answers — words on the glass

Tags are drawn on the window's glass, not placed in the miniature. They are
built when the pick changes, and **placed every frame** from where the lens
has projected what they name:

1. **YOU** sits beside the figure, and keeps off the figure.
2. Each **exit tag** sits beside its gate or doorway ("TO <NAME>", "SHUT --
   TO <NAME>", "A WAY ON, NOT YET WALKED"), on whichever side is free. It
   never covers another exit's mark.
3. The **name and summary block** ("N OPEN   N SHUT   ENTER/A: MORE") sits
   just outside the picked room: above it, else below, else beside.
4. A tag whose subject leaves the window is hidden. The picked place's
   name block hides while its edge mark names it.

`state().tag_clashes` counts tags that cover tags, tags that cover the
picked room, and tags outside the window. It is **0** in every scripted
configuration:
- all nine places of the latched save;
- orbited 45°;
- tilted;
- with the detail open.

### What you look away from stays findable

When the player pans, orbits or zooms away, only these stand at the
window's edge, each with an arrow toward it:

- **YOU**, in ink;
- **the picked place**, by name, in signal;
- **the Journal's link**, ending in its own arrow.

These marks:
- keep clear of each other and of BACK TO YOUR VIEW;
- only ever point at what the save already knows:
  `state().edge_marks_unknown` must be 0;
- never point at a room not found, at where to go next, or at a way the
  player has not walked.

### Four marks, four shapes

| Mark | Shape | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus** | a frame round the room | signal |
| **Circuit** | its symbol on its gate | the circuit's colour, which is Production's and not redefined |
| **You** | the standing figure, plus the YOU tag; off the window, an edge mark | ink |
| **The Journal's link** | a ring of 18 dots, and the link's guide landing on it | ink; the guide in ivory |

None of them is told by hue alone.

### Data bindings

All from `MapFace`:
- `rooms_shown`, `connectors_shown`, `connector_points`, `blockers_shown`;
- `floors`, `band_of_room`, `player_floor`;
- `detail_text` (via `pick`);
- `colours`, `PULSE_HZ`, `PULSE_SWING`.

### Truth rules

- An unknown room is never drawn or named.
- A link into the unknown lands on the **known** end of its connector.
- The overview frames what is known.

## 4. Journal and the LINK

**Composition.** Each column hangs from a run dropped off the trunk (x 46
and x 600), and its headings are flag labels on the run.
- The left column holds OBJECTIVES and NOTES.
- The right column holds the sections that name places: STILL SHUT, WHAT
  YOU DID HERE and PLACES FOUND. They stand against the corner that
  leads to the Map, so the link never crosses other words.
- This is a recomposition of JournalFace's columns (§6.13).
- The focused entry is a **warm tag** clipped to its run, with a SIGNAL
  edge. Its explanation unfolds **in place**, so following the link is
  optional.
- The quieter entries were lifted for 1280 × 720 in the fifth ruling
  (§1).

### States

- `column`: 0 or 1.
- A focus index and a scroll for each column.
- `note`: a one-line account of what changed.

### Transitions

| Input | Effect |
|---|---|
| ↑ ↓, or the d-pad | entries, skipping headings; the focused one unfolds on its tag; the column scrolls to keep it whole; a knock at either end |
| ← →, or the d-pad | columns |
| Enter or A | **SHOW ON THE MAP** (§3): a cue sounds; turn right to the Map, which frames what the entry names, with a way back |
| E or RB | ordinary travel: turn right to the Map as it was left; the link still lands (or points) at its place |
| Click | pick |
| Wheel / right stick | scroll the column by hand |

### The link

**Bound to identity.** It is bound to `{edge: <edge_id>}` for a passage or
`{room: <room_id>}` for a place, never to a row number or the text.

**Its path, as one object across two walls:**
1. **An ivory wire** (`Parts.IVORY`, 8 px thick as seen: `WIRE_R` 4)
   leaves the focused tag's right end.
2. It rises at x 1210 in front of the trunk, and is laced above it (y 16).
3. It rounds the corner post with the trunk, and runs along above the
   Map's own run to over the place it names.
4. It drops forward over the trunk into the port through a metal
   grommet. Lacing ties follow it every 88 px.
5. **In the port, the menu's guide stroke** carries it on: 6 px as seen,
   every turn cut at 45° (12 px), straight down to the passage. It stops
   22 px short, so the passage's mark stays in sight, and ends with a
   terminus bar.
6. The drop and the guide follow the lens every frame.

**Off the view.** The guide stops at the view's edge (`view_rect`, which
excludes an open detail tag) and ends in an arrow, pointing the rest of
the way. The overview brings it back in.

**Not a traversable route.** It is straight between its bends and never
follows a corridor's turns.

**Motion.**
- It is paid out from the tag at an even pace: the wire, then the guide,
  0.42 s, linear.
- A new link rewinds the old one first (0.14 s).
- Only the newest request is ever cast.
- Reduced motion: it is simply there.

**Symbols.** Each linked entry carries its connector's symbol at its end:
- **shut:** the circuit's symbol, in the circuit's colour;
- **open:** the way-out symbol, in ink.

### When a save changes

- The focus **follows the same identity**.
- If the passage's state changed, the note says so, for example
  `NOW OPEN.`, and the explanation's `NOW:` line follows.
- If the passage is not in the new save's journal, the note says `THE
  PASSAGE YOU FOLLOWED IS NOT IN THE JOURNAL NOW.`, and the Map shows no
  link into what that save does not know.

### Data bindings

- `JournalQuery.objectives`, `still_shut`, `done_here`, `places`, `notes`
  and `campaign`.
- The links need identity rows; see §6.2.

## 6. Proposed Production changes

These are proposed Production integration changes. Nothing in Production
is edited until Production is given an explicit integration assignment.

**1–6 were decided by the owner in the second ruling** (2026-09-27):

1. **Reduced motion includes gate pulses and all other secondary motion.**
   - At `a2b9df6` (unchanged at `867f742`), `map_face.gd` `_process` →
     `_pulse()` scales every blocker by
     `1 + PULSE_SWING·sin(t·2π·PULSE_HZ)` unconditionally.
   - MenuShell already reads the setting:
     `PlayerSettings.shared().value("motion_intensity") <= 0.0`.
   - Suggested shape, for Production to write:
     ```gdscript
     func _pulse() -> void:
         var s := 1.0
         if PlayerSettings.shared().value("motion_intensity") > 0.0:
             var t := Time.get_ticks_msec() / 1000.0
             s = 1.0 + PULSE_SWING * sin(t * TAU * PULSE_HZ)
         for eid: String in _blocker_nodes:
             (_blocker_nodes[eid] as Node3D).scale = Vector3.ONE * s
     ```
2. **Journal links consume explicit passage and room identifiers**, not
   identity derived from the words shown. This is a contract request.
   - Today `still_shut`, `done_here` and `places` return strings. To bind
     a link to identity, the extraction re-derives each line and matches it
     exactly, calling JournalQuery's **private** helpers (`_sorted`,
     `_opened_by`, `_in`, `_circuit`).
   - The request is sibling functions that keep the strings unchanged and
     add the identity:
     - `still_shut_rows(snapshot) -> [{text, edge_id, state, circuits}]`
     - `done_here_rows(snapshot) -> [{text, edges: [...], circuits: [...]}]`,
       where edges and circuits may be empty
     - `places_rows(snapshot) -> [{text, room_id}]`
   - The rows keep JournalQuery's existing boundaries: names only from the
     bridge's map, and never an unfound room.
3. **Esc and B back out of an open detail first, then close the menu when
   there is no deeper view. A clear direct resume action stays.**
   - Deeper views, innermost first:
     1. the Map's detail;
     2. the Map's shown view (BACK TO YOUR VIEW);
     3. the Map's pick;
     4. the Equipment rack (back to the keys).
   - The prototype already does this for **B**. It keeps Production's
     **Esc**, which closes at once, because the file is Production's.
     Under the decision, Esc follows B's order and closes only from the
     top.
   - The direct resume stays clear:
     - RESUME on the Pause wall;
     - on the pad, **Start** (Production's `pause`) keeps closing the menu
       at once, whatever is open, since B is the pad's back;
     - on the keyboard, Esc becomes back-then-close, and Tab
       (`inventory`) still closes from Equipment.

     Which keyboard key, if any, should close at once from any depth is
     Production's call to make with this change.
4. **Navigation pressed during a turn does not silently disappear.**
   Production and the prototype drop face input for the 0.42 s turn.
   - Proposed: a direction or scroll pressed during a turn is delivered to
     the face being turned *to*, once it faces the eye.
   - **Never replayed:** an equip, a quit, or any other confirmation
     (Enter / A, a click on an action) pressed during a turn is dropped. It
     is never carried into a control the player has not yet seen.
5. **720p text size is a concern to improve, not a guarantee.**
   - Production's default is 1280 × 720 with no stretch. There, 1 page px
     is about 0.86 screen px, so 2x body text has about 8.6 px caps.
   - The fifth ruling lifted the quieter words (§1). Important text is
     never shrunk to fit: a long name wraps, down to 3x at the least in
     the inspection window, and a long description takes more lines.
   - Recommend a 720p legibility check in Production's own suite.
6. **Dimmed other floors are for overview; a genuine single-floor view is
   kept.**
   - The LENS dims the other floors on its first floor steps, for
     orientation.
   - Production's single-floor view (the others hidden) stays available.
     The proposal is a dimmed overview step, not a replacement.

**Also proposed by the prototype, and approved with it:**

7. **SHOW ON THE MAP and BACK TO YOUR VIEW** (§3). Following a Journal
   entry frames its known target and keeps the previous view; ordinary
   travel never moves the view. Backspace is a proposed new binding on the
   Map. B and the words on the glass are the others.
8. **Edge marks** for YOU and the picked place when they leave the window
   (§3). They point only at known rooms.
9. **The text face's cell is 7 × 8** (Glyph handoff §1): a clear column on
   both sides of every glyph. Nothing Production draws changes: the ink
   and the advances are the same.

**The hybrid itself: the selected menu design.** The sixth ruling
approved its visual direction and the interactions its tour shows:

10. **The walls:** the enclosure's graphite enamel, and one harness round
    all four walls and posts, held by saddle clamps. The titles become
    flag labels on it (TITLE and 0N), replacing MenuShell's title labels
    and page numbers.
11. **Equipment's layout** (§2) replaces EquipmentFace's EQUIPPED / OWNED
    / DETAIL regions:
    - the keys go on a selector with ALWAYS ON;
    - each key has a rack of what fits it, instead of one owned grid;
    - the readout is the detail, with the action at its foot.

    §C.5 lists the parts of EquipmentFace that still need a place.
12. **Settings and Pause's layout** (§5) replaces PauseMenu's centred panel
    and SettingsFace's default controls. The same actions, options,
    ranges, steps and words are set on the salvaged boards.
13. **The Journal's recomposition** (§4): places against the Map's
    corner, per-entry focus with the answer in place, and the link.
    JournalFace today is one scrolled page, without per-entry focus.
14. **Menu sound cues.** Production's menu is silent today. The cues are:
    tick, detent, edge, scroll, page, preview, restore, refuse, value,
    toggle, follow, return, open and close.
    - They are synthesised with Tones' own recipe at 22 050 Hz, and each
      player sits at −12 to −21 dB. The tour's loudest cue peaks at
      −15.5 dBFS.
    - They play on the Master bus, and reduced motion does not silence
      them.
    - They are on the tour's soundtrack, but their levels and timbre are
      first settings that nobody has judged by ear. Where they live, and
      their levels, is §C.9.

**Built into the prototype, but not shown in the tour; proposed, and not
yet ruled on:**

15. **Held-key acceleration on sliders.** From the third repeat within
    0.35 s of the last, each repeat doubles the step (×2, ×4, ×8, ×16), up
    to 20 steps a press. This makes sensitivity's 198 steps reachable with
    a d-pad. The tour steps a slider one press at a time.
16. **MOTION applies at once** (§C.8). The tour switches reduced motion
    from the prototype's harness, not from Settings.

**A data fix, at the source:**

17. **History notes are written for players** (§B, the `+40 max_value`
    note).
